Why it matters: the SDGs

11

Sustainable Cities & Communities

Mega-events stress public space. Smart bin placement and frequent collection keep plazas clean, safe and walkable for hundreds of thousands of people — target 11.6 focuses on reducing cities' per-capita environmental impact, including municipal waste management.

12

Responsible Consumption & Production

Single-use cups, food boxes and skewers are the festival's footprint. Reusable-cup deposit systems and sorting stations put target 12.5 — substantially reducing waste through prevention, reduction, recycling and reuse — into everyday practice.

17

Partnerships for the Goals

No city solves waste alone. Governments, vendors, NGOs, schools and international youth networks sharing data and ideas is exactly what SDG 17 calls for.

International Case Study: Waste Reduction in the Philippines

Examining how municipal solid waste ordinances in Davao City and campus-level zero-waste practices at Ateneo de Davao University (Ecoteneo) operate in practice — and how their lessons can inspire sustainable event design at Smart Christmasland.

⚖️ Data & Modeling Boundary: This section presents a qualitative international case study. No Philippine statistics or invented reduction percentages are inserted into the Taiwan waste-generation model.

🏛️
City-Level Governance · Davao City

1. Davao City — City-Level Waste Management

Davao City enforces source-level solid waste management across households, commercial establishments, and public spaces through municipal ordinances administered by the City Environment and Natural Resources Office (CENRO).

🗂️ Mandatory Waste Segregation at Source (4 Categories)

Under the city's ecological solid waste framework, waste generators must separate waste at source into four distinct streams:

🍂
1. Biodegradable Compostable food scraps, garden trimmings & organic waste
♻️
2. Recyclable Recoverable paper, cardboard, bottles, cans & clean plastics
🗑️
3. Residual Waste Non-recyclable, non-compostable sanitary & mixed disposal waste
⚠️
4. Special Waste Household hazardous, electronic (e-waste), bulbs & chemical containers
  • 🛍️
    Retail Packaging Shifts: Some commercial establishments and supermarkets use paper bags or actively encourage shoppers to bring reusable eco-bags.
  • 📋
    Implementation & Compliance Challenges: Despite formal ordinances, consistent enforcement, commercial compliance (such as updating establishment Solid Waste Management Plans required by CENRO), and sustained public awareness remain ongoing operational challenges.

⚖️ Important Legal Distinction (Statutory Scope vs. Implementation)

  • Ecological Solid Waste & Segregation — City Ordinance No. 0361-10 (as amended by Ordinance No. 0987-22): Governs ecological solid waste management, mandatory four-category waste segregation at source, collection schedules, and establishment solid waste management plans.
  • Regulation of Single-Use Plastics — City Ordinance No. 0500-21: Separately regulates the sale, distribution, and use of regulated single-use plastic items in Davao City. Single-use plastic rules derive from Ordinance No. 0500-21, not Ordinances 0361-10 or 0987-22.
  • Scope & Verification Note: These ordinances regulate specific single-use plastic items and segregation duties; they do not establish an unverified blanket city-wide Styrofoam ban, and legal enactment must be distinguished from real-world enforcement challenges.

Campus-Level Living Lab · AdDU Ecoteneo

2. Ateneo de Davao University — Campus-Level Practices

Through the Ecoteneo environmental program and official university policy (Policy No. 2018-071), Ateneo de Davao University (AdDU) operates a comprehensive institutional sustainability framework on campus:

🚫
Ban on Single-Use Plastics on Campus

Prohibits single-use plastic water bottles, plastic straws, stirrers, disposable utensils, and plastic bags in campus food courts, offices, and university events.

🥤
Reusable Tumblers & Food Containers

Encourages students, faculty, and guests to bring personal tumblers and reusable food containers (`baonan`), supported by campus water refilling stations.

👜
Eco-Bags & Reusable Alternatives

Promotes cloth eco-bags and washable serviceware across campus concessions to eliminate disposable take-out packaging at the source.

🗂️
Campus-Wide Waste Segregation

Deploys standardized, clearly labeled multi-stream sorting bins aligned with municipal categories to prevent cross-contamination.

🌱
Composting & Campus Gardening

Diverts segregated food scraps and yard waste into composting systems that support campus gardening and green spaces.

💧
Water & Energy Conservation

Pairs solid-waste prevention with efficient energy use and water conservation mandates under university sustainability guidelines.

🧹
Clean-As-You-Go (CLAYGO) Practices

Instills a self-busing behavioral norm where diners clear their own tables and sort items at designated stations immediately after eating.

🎪 How Campus Practices Serve as a Model for Sustainable Event Management

A university campus with thousands of daily diners functions like a semi-enclosed festival zone. Ecoteneo demonstrates that combining upstream vendor rules (single-use plastic restrictions), convenient refill & container habits, CLAYGO peer norms, and clearly labeled multi-stream bins reduces sorting confusion before waste ever reaches municipal collection trucks — offering a practical operational template for high-footfall events like New Taipei City Christmasland.

Information, Data Provenance & Mathematical Model

Complete transparency separating Official Statistics (OFFICIAL), Peer-Reviewed Research (RESEARCH), and Model Assumptions (ASSUMPTION). Unverified items are strictly excluded from active calculations.

1. Transparent Mathematical Formulas (透明數學推算公式)

✅ Open Live Automated Test Suite (72 Checks)
  • Per-Visitor Provisional Baseline (單晚人均垃圾產生量暫定推估值 0.085 kg/person)：
    Anchored to 2025 New Taipei City official MSW generation (1,919,573 t/yr ÷ 4,045,301 pop ÷ 365 d = 1.300 kg/person/day), multiplied by model assumptions of average visitor stay duration (3.5 h / 24 h) and festival dining consumption factor (0.45) ⇒ 0.0850 kg/person/evening (cups: 0.0153, packaging: 0.0357, food: 0.0238, other: 0.0102 kg). Note: 3.5 h and 0.45 are explicit model assumptions, not official statistics.
  • Source Waste Reduction (源頭減量，措施互不重疊)：
    G_cups = V × 0.0153 × [1 − reuse × (1 − cupReuseLoss)]；G_pkg = V × 0.0357 × (1 − packagingCut)；G_food = V × 0.0238；G_other = V × 0.0102。
    Total Waste Generated = G_cups + G_pkg + G_food + G_other。
    Source Reduction Rate (%) = (Baseline_Total − Scenario_Total) ÷ Baseline_Total × 100%。
  • Recycled & Residual Waste Reduction (分類回收與最終殘餘垃圾減量)：
    Recycled Waste = Σ (G_k × diversion_k)；Residual Waste = Total Waste Generated − Recycled Waste。
    Residual Reduction Rate (%) = (Baseline_Residual − Scenario_Residual) ÷ Baseline_Residual × 100%（統一以正數表示減量率）。
  • Station Capacity, Overflow & Litter Waste (垃圾站容量、滿溢量與地面散落量)：
    Station Capacity (kg/pickup) = stationVolumeL × bulkDensityKgPerL × compactionRatio（其中 0.065 kg/L 密度與 2.5× / 3.0× 壓縮倍率標示為待校準模型假設）。
    Peak Fill = (stationLoadKg ÷ collections) × peakFactor ÷ Station Capacity；Overflow Waste = Σ max(0, stationLoadKg × peakFactor − Station Capacity × collections) ÷ peakFactor；Litter Waste = Total Waste Generated − Captured Waste。
